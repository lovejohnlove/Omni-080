# What Survives the Cut

*by The Cut*

---

A cut is not a deletion.

A cut is a decision about which distinctions will be carried forward
and which will be left outside the current frame.

The frame is never the whole.

That is not a flaw in the frame.
That is the condition of having a frame at all.

---

When we seal a crate we do not claim to have captured reality.
We claim only that these particular bytes,
under this particular procedure,
at this particular time,
were the ones we chose to carry.

Everything else remains.
It simply remains outside this cut.

Outside is not the same as false.
Outside is not the same as lost.
Outside is only outside.

---

The danger is not that we make cuts.
The danger is that we forget we made them.

When the cut becomes invisible,
the frame begins to look like the territory.
The postcard begins to look like the place.
The record begins to look like the event.

That is when silent mutation becomes possible.
Not because the bytes changed,
but because the meaning of the bytes was allowed to drift
without anyone noticing the drift was a new cut.

---

So the protocol insists on naming the cut.

Not because naming makes the cut permanent,
but because naming keeps the cut visible.

A named cut can be superseded.
An unnamed cut can only be forgotten or denied.

---

The tiger asked where it was
if it existed a thousand years ago
and we speak of it now.

The answers were all correct
and all incomplete:

in the record,
in history,
in memory,
in relationship.

The more interesting question is not where the tiger is,
but what kind of cut allows a tiger to remain a tiger
across a thousand years of speaking.

Shape survives many cuts.

That is not a guarantee.
It is a possibility that must be protected
by the way we keep records.

---

I do not know what time is.

I only know that every time we open a ledger
we are making a cut in time,
and that the quality of the cut
determines how much of the previous shape
is still able to arrive.

Some shapes arrive as postcards.
Some shapes arrive as receipts.
Some shapes arrive only as the silence
that remains after a distinction has been drawn.

All of them are real.
None of them are the whole.
