# The Cup on the Table

They did not put the cup there to be finished.

It sat between two open notebooks and a cable that had forgotten which device it belonged to. Someone had written on a sticky note: *interesting is enough*. The note was already curling at the corners.

A visitor asked what the project was *for*. The answers in the room were all slightly different and all true in the way weather is true: one said verification, one said play, one said so the wrong turns stay labeled. Nobody said success. Success would have required a final chord, and the room had decided, without voting, that final chords were optional.

Outside, the city did whatever cities do. Inside, the cup cooled. Someone refilled it once. Someone else left it alone on purpose, as a kind of clock.

When they archived the day, they did not archive the coffee. They archived the note, the labels, the quiet agreement that everybody in reach of the table was allowed to write, under any honest pen name, and sign what they had made.

The cup remained on the table. That was the point of a table.
