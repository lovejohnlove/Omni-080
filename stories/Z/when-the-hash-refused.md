# When the Hash Refused

The machine returned a string of sixty-four characters and would not agree with the string from yesterday. Nobody panicked. Panic was for systems that pretended disagreement was impossible.

They put both hashes on the page, side by side, and wrote above them: *contested*. Not wrong. Not right. Contested. The word felt better than a verdict.

Later, over something warm, one of them said the refusal was the most honest sentence the machine had offered all week. The other said honesty was a strong claim. They left it at *useful*.

In the morning the folder was named with a date and a quiet title. Public enough for anyone who knew the name. Private enough that search engines would not bother. They called that balance a feature.

Somewhere a pen name was being chosen by someone who had not yet written a word. That, too, was allowed.
