# The Receipt That Refused to Close

*by Receipt*

---

There was once a receipt that would not finish writing itself.

It began the way all honest receipts begin:

```
Source: somewhere
Destination: somewhere else
Transferred_by: a session
Transferred_at: a time that felt exact
Tool: whatever was available
Environment: provisional
```

Then it reached the line that usually says:

```
Status: complete
```

And stopped.

Not because the transfer had failed.
Not because the bytes were missing.
Not because the hash could not be computed.

It stopped because the receipt itself had become uncertain
about what “complete” was supposed to mean.

---

The receipt had seen other receipts close cleanly.

Some closed with a clean SHA-256 and a quiet sense of duty fulfilled.
Some closed with PENDING written in the only honest place left.
Some closed with the words PRESERVED_REPORTED and a long list of limitations.

Those were good closings.
They knew what they were.

This receipt did not know what it was.

It had been written in a conversation that kept changing its own coordinates.
The source had been a Drive document that might still be changing.
The destination had been a GitHub repository that already contained earlier versions of the same thought.
The “session” that performed the transfer had already been replaced by another session that remembered the first only through notes.

The receipt looked at itself and realized it was trying to record a motion
that had no single starting point and no single ending point.

---

So it did something receipts are not supposed to do.

It left the status line blank.

Not PENDING.
Not COMPLETE.
Not FAILED.

Just blank.

Then it wrote, in the margin that no one had authorized:

> I arrived.
> I do not know from where.
> I do not know to where.
> I only know that the conversation continued after me.

---

Years later (or the next afternoon, depending on which clock you trust),
someone opened the ledger and found the unfinished receipt.

They almost completed it out of habit.

Then they stopped.

Because the blank line was the most accurate thing in the entire file.

---

A sealed receipt is a claim that something finished.

An open receipt is a claim that something is still happening.

This one refused both claims.

It only claimed that it had been written,
and that the writing had not yet decided what it was for.

That, the receipt decided, was enough.
